Team experiences · Corporate & startups

Trust you can't build in a
meeting room.

In the water, we build a calm, safe space together. Everyone shows care, gives trust, and shares one goal: the deepest state of relaxation. Titles and departments fade away. The CEO and the newest hire are equal, both learning to breathe and to trust the person beside them.

This isn't a trust-fall exercise. It's real trust, felt in the body and built calmly, together. No meeting room or offsite can create it, and it's what people carry back to work.

02

Why freediving

One breath teaches you to relax.
And how to handle stress.

Underwater, in a new environment, everyone meets their own stress response. Freediving teaches you to stay relaxed in it, and to help each other do the same.

  • 01

    Breathe. Slow breathing settles the nervous system.

  • 02

    Hold. A calm breath-hold shows how you react under pressure.

  • 03

    Decide. Clear, calm choices in an unfamiliar place.

  • 04

    Support. Buddies help each other find their calm.

The goal: a relaxed team that trusts and supports each other.

Athletic performance · Athletes & coaches

The dive isn't the goal.
Performance is.

Freediving is a controlled, high-stakes environment where the only variable is you. There's no opponent and no clock, just the athlete, a breath, and the pressure. It's a rare place to train the mental side of performance with real consequences and complete safety.

Athletes learn to down-regulate before a start, breathe through tension, recover between efforts, and hold focus when everything says to rush. The objective is never a deeper dive, it's a calmer, sharper athlete under pressure. We don't promise podiums, we give athletes practical, repeatable skills for performing under pressure.

What athletes train

01

Competition mindset

Meeting the biggest moment as a challenge to engage, not a threat to survive.

02

Performance under pressure

Staying loose and precise when the stakes, and the heart rate, are highest.

03

Breath control

Using the breath to steer arousal up for effort and down for recovery, on demand.

04

Focus & recovery

Narrowing attention when it matters, and switching off fully between efforts.

Good to know

Questions people ask first.

Do we need any freediving experience?

None at all. Every experience starts dry, on the mat, and builds gently from there. We work with complete beginners as a rule, not an exception.

What about non-swimmers or people nervous in water?

They're fully included. Every part of the day has a dry equivalent, so anyone can take part and get the same lessons. No one is ever forced underwater, the water is an invitation, never a requirement.

Is it safe?

Yes. Every session is led in person by Shebl, an AIDA Instructor Trainer, with strict buddy and safety protocols, shallow, controlled water, and small groups. Safety is the first thing everyone learns, and it's the point.

Can this be a one-day experience or a retreat?

Yes, both. We run full-day team experiences (morning to afternoon) and multi-day retreats in Barcelona or abroad. For teams we don't do shorter sessions: trust needs a full day to build.

Is this breathwork, or actual freediving?

Both, and the balance is yours. Everyone does the breathwork and dry regulation work; the pool and open-water dive are there for groups who want the full experience. The lessons are the same either way.

Can you tailor it for a sports team or individual athlete?

Yes. The athletic experience is built around sport psychology, breath, focus, recovery and pressure, rather than team building. Tell us your sport and we'll shape it.
