Why would a tech team learn to hold its breath?

Because fast-paced work and freediving have one thing in common: pressure.

Deadlines, launches, messages at midnight. In startups and corporates, pressure is the normal state. Most of us answer it the same way. Faster breathing, faster decisions, shorter fuses.

Most team training works on skills and strategy. Almost none trains the state underneath them.

Freediving does. One breath underwater shows you, in real time, how you respond when the pressure rises. Panic, and the air goes fast. Stay calm, and you find more than you thought you had.

And you never do it alone. One person dives, one keeps them safe. Trust stops being a slide in an offsite and becomes something you feel.

That's what a team takes back to work. Calm under pressure, clearer communication, and real trust in the person next to them.

Over the next weeks I'll share what happens when a team breathes, holds and dives together.
