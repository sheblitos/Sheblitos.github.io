In the water, the CEO is just another buddy.

Wetsuits look the same on everyone. Nobody cares about your title when you're face down on one breath and someone else is keeping you safe.

After 160+ students, I've learned one thing about calm: the calmest diver is almost never the one you'd expect. Often it's the quiet one, the person who rarely speaks up.

For a team, that's a gift. Leaders who go first, admit they're nervous, and let a junior colleague keep them safe teach more than any talk about psychological safety.

Hierarchy is useful at work. One breath down, it disappears, and people see each other differently when they come back up.

What would your team learn if you let them keep you safe for a minute?
