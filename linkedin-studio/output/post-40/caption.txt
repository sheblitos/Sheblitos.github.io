70 meters. On one breath.

That's the deepest I've gone. Taller than a 20-storey building, straight down a line and back up again, with no tank.

People ask what it takes. The honest answer: everything.

The body. Flexibility in the chest and diaphragm, equalization, technique, strength. Years of small improvements.

The mind. Staying quiet in a place with completely new rules. No breathing, and gravity pulling you down instead of holding you up. Every thought you bring with you gets louder down there.

The soul. Knowing why you're going. On a deep dive you can't fake it. If you're there for ego, you feel it.

And here's what surprised me. Freediving shows you how you truly react to a new environment, to stress, to discomfort. Then it teaches you to meet them focused, relaxed and calm. Once you can do that at 70 meters, a hard conversation, a big decision or starting over in a new country all feel different.

Going deep myself was one thing. Teaching others to do it was something else. You can't hand someone your depth. You can only help them build their body, their calm and their reasons, one dive at a time.

What have you worked on for years that people only see the result of?
