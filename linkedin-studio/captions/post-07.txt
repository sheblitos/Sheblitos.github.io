The most important decision in a deep dive happens on land.

Before every dive, I set my target depth. Then I stop negotiating.

Underwater, your brain starts bargaining. Pressure builds, the urge to breathe gets loud, and "just two more meters" suddenly sounds reasonable. That's exactly when divers get hurt.

So the rule is simple: decide before the pressure, not during it.

I use the same rule at work now. Budget limits, launch dates, what I'll say no to. I set them when I'm calm, because I know I won't think clearly at the deadline.

Pressure doesn't help you decide better. It only makes you decide faster.

What's one decision you'd rather make before the pressure hits?
