Freediving safety: the systems that make the sport possible

Good freediving safety is not a rescue trick you learn at the end of a course. It is the framework around every dive: planning, buddying, observation and an immediate response when something is wrong.

By Shebl · AIDA Instructor Trainer & Judge · Updated October 2026

Core rule: Never practise breath-hold diving in water alone. A trained buddy must actively supervise the dive and be positioned to assist at the surface.

Why hypoxia matters in freediving

During a breath-hold, oxygen is being consumed while carbon dioxide rises. A diver can feel relatively comfortable and still approach a low-oxygen state. That is why subjective comfort is not a reliable safety system.

LMC and blackout

A loss of motor control (LMC) is a hypoxic event in which the diver may remain conscious but lose coordinated control. A blackout is a loss of consciousness caused by insufficient oxygen reaching the brain. Both require an immediate trained buddy response.

The surface is part of the dive

Safety does not end when the face reaches air. The first moments after surfacing are important, and a buddy should remain attentive while the diver restores normal breathing and demonstrates clear responsiveness.

Why hyperventilation is dangerous

Excessive pre-dive hyperventilation can reduce carbon dioxide and delay the urge to breathe without adding a meaningful oxygen reserve. That can remove an important warning signal and increase risk.

What an effective buddy actually does

A buddy knows the dive plan, watches the final part of the ascent, meets the diver at the appropriate depth, maintains close observation at the surface and is trained to support the airway and respond to an LMC or blackout.

Depth, training and ego

Progression should be based on repeatable technique and recovery, not on forcing a personal best. Turning around early is a normal safety decision. Good freedivers protect the quality of the next dive rather than gambling everything on the current one.

Learn rescue skills in person

Reading about safety is useful, but rescue technique is a practical skill. It should be taught and rehearsed with a qualified freediving instructor so the response becomes familiar before it is ever needed.
